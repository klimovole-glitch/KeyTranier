## Morning in the City
The city wakes up early. The first buses leave the depot, street cleaners sweep the sidewalks, and lights come on in the windows of small bakeries. People hurry to work with cups of hot coffee in their hands. In an hour the streets will be full of noise, but right now you can hear the birds singing in the yard.

## Touch Typing
Touch typing lets you write text without looking at the keyboard. Each finger is responsible for its own group of keys, and the hands always return to the home row. At first your speed will be low, but after a few weeks of regular practice the movements become natural and you stop thinking about where each letter is.

## The Autumn Forest
In October the forest looks like a painting. Birch trees stand in yellow, maple leaves turn bright red, and the dark pines seem even more serious. Leaves rustle under your feet, and the air smells of mushrooms and wet earth. If you stop and listen, you can hear a woodpecker somewhere high in the branches.

## How to Learn Something New
To master a new skill, it helps to break it into small steps. Spend at least twenty minutes on it every day instead of several hours once a week. Pay attention to your mistakes and work on them first. Then you will notice real progress within a month.

## The River
A river begins as a small stream somewhere in the mountains. At first it runs fast and loud, jumping over stones and foaming on the rapids. Then it reaches the plain, becomes wider and calmer, and slowly flows past fields and villages. At the end of its journey it meets the sea and gives it all the water collected along the way.

## Winter Morning
Snow fell during the night and covered the yard with a smooth white blanket. The first person outside leaves a line of footprints from the door to the road. The air is fresh and sharp, and it is easy to breathe. Somewhere in the distance a street cleaner scrapes the pavement with a shovel, and that sound seems to be the only one in the whole city.

## The Sea
The sea is rarely the same. In the morning it is calm and grey like tin, and only a light ripple runs across the surface. In the afternoon the wind raises waves, and they roll onto the shore with a loud noise, leaving a wet line on the sand. By evening the water calms down again and reflects the sunset, looking like a stretched canvas.

## The House by the Road
A small house stood right by the road, and everyone who drove to the city passed it. The owner kept pots of geraniums in the window, and an old dog always lay on the porch. No one remembered when the house had appeared there, but it seemed it had always been there, like a tree or a stone by the roadside.

## The Train
The train left at seven in the morning, when the lights on the platform were still on. People waved goodbye, and faces flashed in the windows of the carriages. Fields, small woods and little stations where nobody got off drifted past the window. By noon the train was far from the city, and passengers were taking food and books out of their bags.

## The Bookshop
The bookshop smelled of paper and dust. Shelves ran from floor to ceiling, and in some places books stood in two rows. The owner knew every one of them and could find the right book even if it was pushed into the farthest corner. Customers came in rarely, but each of them stayed for a long time.

## Morning in the Village
The rooster wakes the village before the sun. The women are already lighting the stoves, and smoke rises above the roofs. Cows walk to the pasture, their bells ringing, and the shepherd follows them without hurry. The air smells of dew and cut grass, and the day promises to be long and calm.

## How to Read Faster
Reading speed depends not only on your eyes but also on your habits. It helps to stop pronouncing words silently and to learn to take in whole phrases at a glance. Start with simple texts and gradually move on to harder ones. In a few weeks you will notice that you read more in the same amount of time.

## The Starry Sky
At night, outside the city, you can see far more stars than in town. They are scattered across the whole sky, and if you look long enough you begin to recognize familiar constellations. Somewhere up there, light has been travelling to us for many years, and we see it as something quite different from what it once was.

## The Old Book
The book was old, with a worn cover and yellowed pages. On the first page someone had written their name long ago, and the ink had already faded. It was hard to read, but every page seemed like a door into another world, where time moves more slowly and things matter more than they seem to.

## The Wind
The wind rose towards evening and drove heavy clouds across the sky. It tore the last leaves from the trees and carried them across the road, then threw them onto the wet asphalt. People turned up their collars and walked faster, but the wind did not fall behind, pushing at their backs and whistling in the wires.

## Silence
Sometimes it is useful to just sit in silence. No phone, no music, no talking. At first it will feel uncomfortable, but gradually your thoughts settle, and you begin to hear the clock ticking and the city breathing outside the window. Decisions often come in such silence that could not be found in noise.

## Journey to the North
People go to the north for light and space. It stays light there for a long time, and even at night the sky remains bright. Roads run through forests and swamps, and for many kilometres around you will not meet a single house. People here live unhurriedly and value what they have.

## Autumn Rain
The rain started in the morning and went on all day. It beat on the roofs, ran down the windows and gathered in puddles on the asphalt. On such days it is good to sit at home with a cup of tea and watch everything outside turn grey and blurred, as if painted in watercolour.

## Short Notes
Sometimes one phrase is enough to remember a thought. You do not have to write long texts — you can keep short notes, observations, ideas. In a month you will reread them and see how much passed by before you started to notice.

## The Old Bridge
The bridge over the river was old and creaked underfoot. The boards were rotten in places, and dark water could be seen through the cracks. Yet every day people walked across it — some to work, some to the market, some just for a walk. The bridge held on, although everyone had long said it was time to replace it.

## First Snow
The first snow always comes unexpectedly. In the morning the ground is still black, and by evening everything around is white and quiet. Children run out into the yard and make snowballs, while adults look out of the windows and remember their childhood. In a few days the snow will melt, but that no longer matters.

## Birds
In autumn birds gather in flocks and fly south. They fly high, and their calls can be heard far around. People raise their heads and watch them go, thinking that one day they too might want to fly away. But the birds return every spring, and that gives hope.

## Evening in the City
Towards evening the city changes. Windows light up, shop windows grow brighter, and the streets fill with people hurrying home. Some stop at a cafe, some just walk, enjoying the cool air. At such moments the city seems to live a life of its own, independent of us.

## The Letter
The letter arrived in an old envelope, with a stamp that no longer exists. Inside was a sheet of paper covered in small handwriting. It was hard to read, but every line seemed important. The writer spoke of things that had long passed, but in his words the life he remembered was still alive.

## Mountains
Mountains seem motionless, but only at first glance. Snow on the peaks melts and freezes again, stones slowly crumble, and rivers carve new paths for themselves. People come to the mountains to feel small and to find the peace they cannot find in the city.

## The Road Home
The road home always seems shorter than the road away from home. Even if you take the same route, your steps become faster and your thoughts calmer. Around the corner you can already see the familiar house, and a light is on in the window. This feeling is familiar to anyone who has ever returned after a long day.

## The Garden
The garden was neglected, but much still grew in it. Apple trees stood in a row, and currant bushes wound between them. The grass was tall, and flowers that no one had planted hid in it. In summer it smelled of honey and wild strawberries, and time seemed to stand still.

## The Cat
The cat lived on the roof and was afraid of no one. By day he slept on the warm tiles, and at night he went wandering through the yards. People left food for him, but he rarely took it — he liked simply to sit nearby and watch them go about their business. No one knew where he had come from.

## Words
Words have power. Sometimes a single word can change a mood, and sometimes a whole life. We rarely think about what we say, but it is worth remembering that behind every word there is a person. Speak so that you will not have to regret what you said.

## Time
Time passes unnoticed. It seems there are many days ahead, but they pass one after another, and a year is already behind you. What matters is not so much counting time as filling it with what truly matters. Then it will not feel lost.

## Water
Water takes any shape but remains itself. It can be calm like a lake and wild like a mountain river. It is needed by all living things, and without it there would be no forests, no fields, no cities. Yet we rarely think about how important it is.

## Sleep
Sleep is not just rest. During sleep the brain processes what happened during the day and puts thoughts in order. That is why it is so important to sleep enough and to go to bed at the same time. Good sleep makes us calmer and more attentive.

## Laughter
Laughter is contagious. When someone laughs sincerely, it is hard not to smile back. Laughter relieves tension and brings people closer. It is no wonder they say laughter prolongs life — and these are not just words.

## A Quiet Town
In a small town everyone knows each other. There are no big shops or noisy streets, but there is a market where fresh vegetables are sold and a park where families walk in the evenings. Life here moves slowly, and that is its main advantage.

## Summer Evening
A summer evening lasts a long time. The sun sets slowly, and the sky turns pink and then violet. The air smells of grass and dust, and somewhere in the distance music can be heard. Such evenings are worth remembering, because they do not come as often as they seem to.

## The Path
Everyone has their own path. Some walk fast and straight, some turn aside and come back. What matters is not so much how quickly you arrive as what you see along the way. Sometimes the longest path turns out to be the most important.
